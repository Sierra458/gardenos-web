# 💜 Lavender

**Variety:** Likely English lavender (*Lavandula angustifolia*) based on label  
**Type:** Mediterranean perennial herb  
**Plant Count:** 3–4 clumps in a rectangular planter  
**Container:** Long black rectangular planter on herb station upper shelf  
**Location:** Herb pipe table, partial-to-full sun  
**Mulch:** Wood chip / bark mulch (better than straw for lavender — drains well)  

---

## Latest Observation — 2026-05-17

**Mixed performance across the planter.** Two clumps show good silvery-green foliage and have produced **purple flower spikes** (one prominent stalk in the photo). The other clumps are showing significant **browning and dieback** — dry crispy leaves, especially on the lower portions. The rightmost clump looks particularly stressed.

## Health Status

🟠 **Poor to fair — half of the plants are stressed/dying, half are recovering and flowering**

## Observations / Concerns

- **Lavender struggles in Houston.** It's a Mediterranean plant that wants dry, alkaline, well-drained soil — Houston gives it humid, acidic, often waterlogged conditions.
- The browning is consistent with either:
  1. **Root rot from too much moisture** (most likely culprit in Houston)
  2. **Winter dieback** that hasn't been pruned out yet
  3. **Salt buildup** from synthetic fertilizers (Miracle-Gro?)
- Good signs: New silvery growth on healthy clumps, flower production on at least 2 stems
- Bark mulch is correct choice (much better than straw for lavender)

## Recommended Actions

**Immediate:**
- [ ] **Prune aggressively** — cut all brown/dead growth back to where you see green or silver wood. Don't cut into bare brown wood (it won't resprout).
- [ ] Check planter drainage — if water pools, drill more holes or add more grit/sand to soil
- [ ] **Stop fertilizing entirely** — lavender hates rich soil. Fertilizer is making it worse.
- [ ] Water only when soil is bone dry 3" down — much less than other plants

**Ongoing:**
- [ ] Apply a top-dressing of crushed oyster shell or garden lime to raise pH (lavender prefers alkaline 6.5–7.5)
- [ ] Add coarse builder's sand or perlite to topsoil to improve drainage further
- [ ] After flowering ends, prune flowers off + give plants a "haircut" to encourage bushy growth

**Houston-specific reality check:**
- Most lavender varieties last 1–2 years in Houston before declining
- *Lavandula x intermedia* "Provence" and *L. dentata* (French lavender) handle humidity better than English
- Consider replacing failing clumps with **rosemary** — same look, same care, but actually thrives in Houston

## Watch for

- Spittlebugs (foamy spit on stems) — rinse off with hose
- Fungal stem rot at base — pull affected plants immediately to save the others
- Spider mites in dry weather — strong water spray on leaves
