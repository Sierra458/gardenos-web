## Observations / Concerns

- **Wrong season for Houston:** Onions in Zone 9a should be started in Oct–Jan for May–June harvest. Starting now means they'll struggle through summer heat and may bolt without forming bulbs.
- All shoots are weak/spindly — indicates either too-low light at germination, overcrowded planting cells, or weak seed
- Excessive straw mulch around onions can smother them — these need exposed soil and full sun on the tops
- The plant labels mention they should be at the **edges** of the raised bed to avoid competition

## Recommended Actions

**Immediate:**
- [ ] Pull straw mulch completely off the onion crowns — they need light on their green tops
- [ ] Don't expect bulb formation this round; treat as a green-onion / scallion crop
- [ ] Harvest as scallions when shoots reach 6–8" — pull whole plant or snip greens
- [ ] Plant new onion sets (small bulbs from nursery) in October for proper spring 2027 harvest

**Strategy decision:**
- **Option A** — Let them grow as scallions, harvest in 4–6 weeks, then replant the space with a heat-tolerant crop (basil, okra, or okra seedlings)
- **Option B** — Pull them now and use the space for something more productive

## Notes for Future Seasons

- **Houston onion calendar:** Start seeds late October, transplant in December–January, harvest May–June
- **Alternative:** Buy onion sets (small bulbs) in January for faster spring crop
- **Sweet Spanish** needs longer day length than Houston provides; switch to short-day varieties like *Texas Grano 1015Y* (the legendary "Texas sweet")
