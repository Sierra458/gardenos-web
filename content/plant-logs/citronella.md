# 🌿 Citronella Plant

**Variety:** Pelargonium citrosum ("Citronella Mosquito Plant" / scented geranium)  
**Type:** Tender perennial (annual in cold climates, perennial in Houston)  
**Plant Count:** 1  
**Container:** Brown plastic pot on herb station shelf  
**Location:** Herb pipe table, partial shade  
**Mulch:** Straw  

---

## Latest Observation — 2026-05-17

The citronella has put on **significant new growth** at the top — fresh light-green frilly leaves on a single central stem reaching upward. The lower portion of the plant shows woody/dead stems and some brown crispy foliage, while new vegetation is concentrated at the apex.

This is a classic recovery pattern — the plant lost older foliage to winter/transplant stress and is now regrowing from the top.

## Health Status

🟡 **Fair → improving — new growth is healthy, lower plant needs cleanup**

## Observations / Concerns

- Dead/woody lower stems should be pruned to redirect energy to new growth
- Plant label "Citronella Mosquito Plant" visible on the side
- Straw mulch is heavy around the base — may be holding too much moisture against the crown
- Note: True citronella oil comes from *Cymbopogon nardus* (a grass). This "citronella" geranium contains citronellol but is **not nearly as effective** at repelling mosquitoes as marketed. Still a nice patio plant.

## Recommended Actions

**Immediate:**
- [ ] Prune dead/woody lower stems with clean shears — cut back to where you see green growth
- [ ] Pull straw mulch back ~3" from the crown — these plants prefer their crown exposed
- [ ] Check pot drainage — lift pot and confirm water drains freely from bottom holes
- [ ] Apply diluted fish emulsion (1 tbsp/gallon) — light feed once monthly

**Ongoing care:**
- [ ] Let soil dry between waterings — these are drought-tolerant once established
- [ ] Pinch growing tips occasionally to encourage bushier growth
- [ ] Move to brighter location if possible — they like 6+ hours of sun

**Watch for:**
- Yellowing leaves → overwatering (most common issue)
- White cottony patches on stems → mealybugs (wipe with rubbing alcohol on cotton swab)
- Fungal leaf spots → cut affected leaves, improve airflow
