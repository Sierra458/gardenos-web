# 🥔 Potato Tower

**Variety:** Seed potato (variety not recorded — confirm if known)  
**Type:** Annual tuber crop (Solanaceae)  
**Container:** White plastic clothes hamper with oval ventilation holes  
**System:** Layered soil/hay vertical grow — add layers as plants grow taller

## Latest Observation — 2026-05-17

**The potato tower is the star of the outdoor garden right now.** Multiple healthy plants are emerging from both the top opening and through the side holes of the hamper. Foliage is vigorous, dark green, no yellowing or pest damage visible.

Count: At least **5–6 distinct emergence points** visible across top and sides.

## Health Status

🟢 **Excellent — vigorous growth, system working as designed**

## Observations / Concerns

- The layered tower system is doing exactly what it should — plants are reaching out through every available opening for light
- Plants on the side will produce tubers in the layer below them — this is the magic of vertical potato growing
- Surface looks adequately moist; straw mulch on top retaining moisture well
- No signs of blight, beetle damage, or wilting

## Recommended Actions

**Ongoing maintenance:**
- [ ] As top plants grow another 6–8", add another 4–6" layer of soil/straw mix to cover the lower stems (this is "hilling" — drives more tuber formation)
- [ ] Continue adding layers until plants reach the top of the hamper or stop growing
- [ ] Water deeply 2–3× per week; potatoes need consistent moisture but hate soggy soil
- [ ] Do NOT fertilize with high nitrogen — this encourages foliage at the expense of tubers
- [ ] Optional: apply fish emulsion (2 tbsp/gallon) once every 3 weeks for balanced nutrition

**Harvest timeline:**
- Foliage will yellow and die back in 70–90 days from sprouting (late June to mid-July)
- When 50% of foliage has died back, **stop watering** for 2 weeks to firm up skins
- Then dump the hamper out onto a tarp and dig through layers to harvest

**Watch for:**
- Colorado potato beetles (yellow/black striped beetles) — hand-pick or use neem
- Early blight (dark brown spots on lower leaves) — Houston humidity makes this likely
- Tubers showing through soil — cover immediately (sunlight turns them green/toxic)
