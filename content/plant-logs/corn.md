## Observations / Concerns

- 4 stalks in one container is borderline crowded — corn needs cross-pollination from at least a 4×4 block ideally, but 4 in close proximity will partially work
- Wind pollination is the main mechanism — gentle hand-shaking of tassels (when they form) helps ensure all ears get pollinated
- Straw mulch looks great, no contact issues
- Single-pot corn is unusual but workable as long as it's deep enough — looks like it is

## Recommended Actions

**Immediate:**
- [ ] Water deeply 1×/day during hot weather — corn is a thirsty crop
- [ ] Top-dress with compost or apply fish emulsion (2 tbsp/gallon) weekly — corn is a *heavy* nitrogen feeder
- [ ] Once stalks hit ~24" tall, side-dress with high-N organic fertilizer (blood meal, alfalfa meal)

**Pollination window (2–4 weeks out):**
- [ ] When tassels (male flowers at top) and silks (female, from emerging ears) appear, **gently shake stalks daily** to release pollen onto silks
- [ ] Each silk strand connects to one kernel — incomplete pollination = gappy ears
- [ ] Consider hand-collecting tassel pollen in a paper bag and brushing onto silks for full pollination

**Watch for:**
- Earworms (caterpillars at the tip of ears) — drop a few drops of vegetable oil into the silks once they brown
- Wind damage — if stalks lean, hill up soil around bases for support
- Aphids on tassels — usually self-resolves with ladybug predation

## Harvest

- Sweet corn is ready when silks turn brown and dry, kernels are plump, and a punctured kernel releases milky liquid (not clear, not doughy)
- Twist + pull down to harvest
- Eat within hours of picking for peak sweetness (sugars convert to starch fast)
