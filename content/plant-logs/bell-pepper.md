## Latest Observation — 2026-05-17

The two bell peppers are showing very different growth rates — one is well ahead of the other.

- **BP1 (foreground, Image 10):** Strong, robust seedling with thick stem, ~4–5" tall, four glossy dark-green leaves, healthy waxy cuticle. **This is the textbook ideal.**
- **BP2 (background/Image 11):** Noticeably smaller, ~3" tall, lighter green leaves, thinner stem. Likely under-developed at transplant or experiencing more shock.

## Health Status

- **BP1:** 🟢 **Excellent** — best-looking transplant in the whole bed
- **BP2:** 🟡 **Fair** — alive but lagging significantly

## Observations / Concerns

- Both have straw mulch — appropriate distance from BP1's stem, slightly close on BP2
- No pest damage, no discoloration beyond the size difference
- Blue plant label visible reading "Bell Pepper"
- Peppers actually prefer the Houston heat more than tomatoes — these should accelerate once temps climb

## Recommended Actions

**Immediate:**
- [ ] Pull mulch back 1–2" from BP2's stem
- [ ] Apply fish emulsion drench to both (2 tbsp/gallon)
- [ ] If Myco Supreme wasn't applied at transplant, drench 1 tbsp/gallon around each root zone
- [ ] Water deeply 1×/day, ~3/4 gallon per plant

**Within 2 weeks:**
- [ ] Pinch off first flower buds on both plants to encourage bushier growth
- [ ] Begin weekly fish emulsion feedings
- [ ] Plants do not need cages until they start setting fruit — current cages are fine

**Watch for:**
- Aphids on new growth (common in Houston May–June)
- Sunscald on fruit once they start setting (the shade cloth plan helps here too)
- Blossom drop above 90°F daytime / 75°F nighttime — peppers are more heat-tolerant than tomatoes but still drop flowers in extreme heat
