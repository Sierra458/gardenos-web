## Health Status

🟡 **Fair — alive but small, container is oversized, may struggle**

## Observations / Concerns

- **Container is too large for a single small seedling.** Excess soil holds excess water → root rot risk. The roots can't dry out the bulk soil → cold, anaerobic conditions.
- White wall behind it will reflect heat and light — good for melon ripening later, but currently may overheat a small plant
- Watermelons need full sun (8+ hours) and 2–3 ft of horizontal sprawl per plant — this pot will need a trellis or the vine will trail across the patio
- No flowering yet — expected at 5–7 weeks from transplant

## Recommended Actions

**Immediate:**
- [ ] Water sparingly until the plant fills more of the pot — soak only when top 2" of soil are dry
- [ ] Pull straw mulch back 2" from the stem
- [ ] Apply fish emulsion drench (2 tbsp/gallon) — melons are heavy feeders
- [ ] Install a trellis or A-frame within 2 weeks before vines start running

**Within 2 weeks:**
- [ ] Train the main vine onto trellis as it grows
- [ ] Once first true vine appears, side-dress with compost
- [ ] Begin weekly feeding with balanced organic fertilizer

**Watch for:**
- Squash vine borers (orange/black wasp-like moths laying eggs at base of stem)
- Powdery mildew on leaves (white dusty coating) — common in Houston humidity
- Yellow blossoms but no fruit set → likely no pollinators reaching the plant; may need to hand-pollinate male → female flowers
- Fruits forming — they'll need a sling (old t-shirt or pantyhose) tied to the trellis to support their weight

**Houston timing note:** A May 17 watermelon transplant gives ~70 days to fruit set, putting first harvest around mid-August. Watermelons handle Houston heat well but need consistent water during fruit swelling.
