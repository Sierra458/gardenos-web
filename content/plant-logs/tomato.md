## Observations / Concerns

- Plants are noticeably smaller than expected for May 17 in Zone 9a — Houston tomatoes should be 8–12" tall and starting to flower by now
- Straw mulch is pulled in close around some stems (rot/pest risk — pull back ~2")
- Slight purple stem tinge on T3 (P-deficiency or UV stress)
- Cages already in place ✓
- No signs of pest damage, fungal disease, or yellowing
- Soil appears moist with good drainage

## Recommended Actions

**Immediate (this week):**
- [ ] Pull straw mulch back 2" from each stem to prevent stem rot and pest harborage
- [ ] Side-dress with compost or apply fish emulsion (2 tbsp/gallon, water in) — gentle organic feed to push growth
- [ ] Water deeply 1×/day in early morning, ~1 gallon per plant
- [ ] If Myco Supreme wasn't applied at transplant, do a 1 tbsp/gallon drench now around each root zone

**Next 2 weeks:**
- [ ] Install shade cloth (40–50%) before first 95°F day — likely late May
- [ ] Pinch off any flower buds that appear before plants reach 12" tall (energy to roots/foliage first)
- [ ] Begin weekly fish emulsion feedings until fruit set
- [ ] Tie main stems loosely to cages as they grow

**Watch for:**
- Yellowing lower leaves (early blight, common in Houston humidity)
- Hornworm damage starting in June
- Blossom-end rot on first fruits (calcium uptake issue from inconsistent watering)
