# 🍋 Lime Tree

**Variety:** Persian or Kaffir lime (confirm — leaves are single-blade, suggesting Persian; Kaffir has distinctive double-lobed leaves)  
**Type:** Tropical citrus tree (perennial)  
**Plant Count:** 1  
**Container:** Large black plastic pot (~10 gallon)  
**Location:** Patio, partial sun against house wall  
**Mulch:** Straw  

---

## Latest Observation — 2026-05-17

The lime tree is **thriving**. Dense, glossy dark-green foliage forms a full canopy with vigorous upward growth and good lateral branching. Fresh light-green new growth visible at the apex indicates active flushing.

**Visible issues:**
- **Slime/shine on leaves** — some surface glossiness consistent with slug or snail trails (Matt's direct observation)
- **Leaf cupping and curling** on multiple branches — distinct from slug damage, classic citrus leaf miner pattern (larvae tunnel inside leaf, visible as silvery squiggly lines, leaves curl protectively)
- A few leaves show edge browning / tip burn — could be salt buildup or under-watering history

## Health Status

🟢 **Excellent — but actively under pest pressure**

## Pest Diagnosis

Likely **two concurrent issues**:

1. **Citrus leaf miner** (*Phyllocnistis citrella*) — endemic in Houston, attacks new flush growth, causes the cupped/curled leaves and silver tunnel patterns. The tree outgrows this; cosmetic damage only.
2. **Slugs or snails** — surface slime on lower leaves. They climb up trunks at night, especially when straw mulch stays damp.

## Recommended Actions

**Slug/snail control:**
- [ ] Apply iron phosphate bait (Sluggo, Garden Safe brand) sprinkled around pot base — safe for pets/wildlife
- [ ] Wrap pot rim with copper tape as a barrier
- [ ] Alternative/supplement: shallow dish of beer at ground level overnight
- [ ] Confirm with flashlight check at dusk
- [ ] Pull straw mulch back from the trunk and thin out the layer — slugs love damp dense mulch

**Citrus leaf miner control:**
- [ ] Spray neem oil weekly (1 tbsp per gallon water + a drop of dish soap to emulsify) during new growth flush periods only
- [ ] Time sprays for evening when bees aren't active
- [ ] Don't remove damaged leaves — the tree still photosynthesizes through them
- [ ] Repeat for next flush in summer

**General citrus care:**
- [ ] Apply Espoma Citrus-tone (or similar citrus-specific fertilizer) monthly Mar–Oct
- [ ] Deep water weekly — let top 2" dry between waterings
- [ ] Plan a **15–20 gallon pot upgrade** within the next year as the tree fills the current pot
- [ ] Strong hose spray every few days to disrupt pest egg-laying and dislodge crawlers

## Watch for

- Citrus rust mite (bronzed fruit later in season)
- Asian citrus psyllid (vector for citrus greening disease, devastating if present) — small mottled-wing insects on new growth
- Yellowing leaves with green veins → micronutrient deficiency (zinc, iron, manganese) — citrus-specific fertilizer addresses this
- Flower buds → confirm pollination

## Care Notes

- **Watering:** Deep weekly soaking, let top 2" dry between waterings
- **Light:** Full sun, 8+ hours
- **Feeding:** Citrus-tone or fish + kelp emulsion monthly Mar–Oct
- **Pruning:** Light pruning after fruit harvest; remove suckers from below graft union
- **Cold protection:** Sensitive below 40°F — move under cover or wrap during Houston winter cold snaps
