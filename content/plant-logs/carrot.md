## Postmortem — Likely Causes of Death

1. **Wrong season for Houston (most likely cause).** Carrots are a cool-season crop. In Zone 9a they should be direct-sown September–February for winter/spring harvest. Sowing in April–May puts germination right into 85°F+ soil temperatures, which carrots refuse to germinate in.

2. **Inconsistent moisture during germination.** Carrot seeds need 14–21 days of *constantly* moist soil to germinate. A single dry-out kills the entire batch. Containers in Houston sun dry out in hours.

3. **Container choice.** A 5-gallon bucket is fine for depth (carrots need 10–12" loose soil), but the orange color absorbs heat, baking the root zone. Black or terra cotta is even worse — light-colored or fabric pots stay cooler.

4. **Possible burial under mulch.** Carrot seeds are tiny and must be sown only 1/4" deep. Heavy straw on top can prevent emergence entirely.

## Lessons Learned

- ❌ Don't direct-sow carrots in Houston after February
- ❌ Don't bury fine seeds under heavy mulch
- ❌ Don't let container surface dry out during germination
- ✅ Cover newly-sown seed with thin board or burlap to keep soil moist (remove on first emergence)
- ✅ Use vermiculite (not straw) as a light cover layer for tiny seeds
