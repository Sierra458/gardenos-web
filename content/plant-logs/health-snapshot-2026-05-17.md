## Top 5 Immediate Actions (this week)

1. **Pull straw mulch back 2" from EVERY stem** in the raised bed. Stem rot risk is real in Houston humidity.
2. **Fish emulsion drench** (2 tbsp/gallon, ~1 cup per plant) for tomatoes, peppers, watermelon, corn.
3. **Aggressive prune of lavender** — cut all brown/dead growth back to green/silver living wood.
4. **Uncover onion crowns** — pull mulch completely off them so green tops get full light.
5. **Repurpose orange carrot bucket** — plant 1 okra or 3 bush bean seeds for a heat-tolerant replacement.

---

## Top 3 Strategic Concerns

### 1. Tomato/Pepper transplants are smaller than ideal
For May 17 in Houston, your tomatoes should be 8–12" with first flowers forming. They're at 3–5". This is fixable with consistent feeding, but every week now is a week stolen from your harvest window. **Window before peak heat shuts down fruit set: ~6 weeks.**

### 2. Pest pressure on lime tree
Citrus leaf miner is hitting the new flush growth (cupped/curled leaves with silver squiggle trails). Slugs or snails are also climbing up the trunk at night, leaving slime on the lower leaves. Neither will kill an established tree, but ignored they hit fruit production. Treatment plan in the lime tree log.

### 3. Multiple plants showing transplant-shock smallness
Tomatoes, peppers, onions, and watermelon all look under-developed. Common cause: insufficient hardening-off duration before transplant, OR transplant during a hot day rather than evening. Both are recoverable with feed + steady water + shade.
