> Status snapshot of the indoor seed-starting nursery for [[Garden Monitor]]. Seeds planted **2026-04-14**, four trays of 12 cells each on the [[Nursery System Design|SOLIGT 5-tier shelf]]. Garage location, Houston.

## Shelf-by-Shelf Layout

| Shelf | Tray | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| **Shelf 4 (top)** | Big container (12 cells) + egg carton | 2 bell pepper, 2 jalapeño, 2 tomato, 4 onion · egg carton: assorted peppers/tomatoes/jalapeños in eggshell halves |
| **Shelf 3** | 12-cell tray | Bell pepper, tomato, onion (4 each) |
| **Shelf 2 left** | 12-cell tray | Corn (Golden Bantam), broccoli (Waltham), carrot (Scarlet Nantes) |
| **Shelf 2 right** | 12-cell tray | Oregano, rosemary, watermelon |
| **Shelf 1 (bottom)** | 12-cell tray | Three lettuce varieties: Buttercrunch Butterhead, Ruby Red, Black Seeded Simpson |

Layout is intentional: heat rises inside the PVC cover, so warm-season crops (peppers, tomatoes, jalapeños) sit on the upper shelves and lettuce stays at the bottom where it's coolest. Natural temperature gradient = no second heat mat needed.

## ⚠️ Heat Issue — Resolved

The garage greenhouse hit **37°C / 98.6°F** at the top shelf during peak afternoon — caused by Houston ambient + grow lights + sealed PVC cover. Lettuce went thermodormant; top-shelf seeds likely got cooked.

**Temperature timeline:**

| Reading | Conditions | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 37.0°C / 98.6°F | Top shelf, afternoon, cover closed | Too hot for everything — even peppers stress at 95°F+ |
| 33.3°C / 91.9°F | Bottom shelf, afternoon, covers off | Still too hot for lettuce (thermodormancy >75°F) |
| 25.2°C / 77.4°F | Bottom shelf, 7:20 AM, covers off, 70% RH | Workable for most crops; borderline for lettuce |

**Mitigations applied:**
- PVC cover **fully off during the day**, only on at night if garage drops below 65°F.
- Box fan pointed at shelves for airflow — drops effective temp 5–10°F.
- Hygrometer moved to bottom shelf to track lettuce zone.
- **Top-shelf big container declared dead** — focus shifted to the strong Shelf 3 tray.

## Eggshell Planters Note

Many top-shelf cells use eggshell halves with cracked tops sitting in a cardboard egg carton. The shells are essentially mini sealed pots — moisture stays in much longer than bare cardboard. Adjustments:

- **Reduce misting to once daily** (vs twice for plastic cells).
- **1–2 teaspoons max per shell** per misting — eggshells don't drain.
- **Poke a pin hole in the bottom of each shell** for drainage and to enable bottom-watering from the carton.
- **Bonus at transplant:** crack the shell more around the sides and **plant the whole thing**. Eggshell decomposes and releases calcium carbonate → prevents tomato blossom-end rot.

## Watering Schedule

See [[Watering Guide]] for the full per-shelf rules. Quick version: cells should feel like a wrung-out sponge (damp throughout, never wet). Water daily on Shelf 4 (driest), every 1–2 days on Shelves 1–3, twice daily on the egg carton.

## Myco Supreme

**Don't apply yet.** Mycorrhizal fungi need active root contact to colonize. Hold until **transplant day** for tomatoes, peppers, jalapeños, watermelon, corn — sprinkle 1 Tbsp into each planting hole. Outdoor lavender/citronella/spearmint can get a dose **now** (already-established root systems). See [[Transplant Timeline]] for dates.

## What's Working / Not Working

**Working:**
- Tier strategy — heat-loving crops up top, lettuce on bottom = natural temp zoning.
- Shelf 3 tray (peppers/tomatoes/onions) is the breakout success.
- Eggshell technique for tomatoes (calcium pre-loading).

**Not working:**
- Top shelf got too hot before vents went on — likely lost the big container and most of the egg carton.
- Lettuce on Shelf 1 was at the edge until temps came down — even now sprouting is slow and uneven.
- Watermelon got leggy (too far from light) — see fix in [[Transplant Timeline#Watermelon Stem Mounding Fix]].
