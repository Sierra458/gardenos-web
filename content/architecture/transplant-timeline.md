## The Rule

A seedling is ready to transplant when it has **4–6 true leaves** AND the stem is thick enough to support upright growth. Cell-bound (roots circling the bottom) means **overdue**.

## Watermelon — Stem Mounding Fix

The watermelon stems got leggy (too thin to support seed leaves; some fell over). Cause: light wasn't close enough during the high-heat days. Fix:

- **Mound soil up around fallen stems**, burying partway up to just below the seed leaves.
- Watermelon (like tomatoes) **grows roots from buried stem** — this actually helps long-term root mass.
- Lower the grow light to **2–3 inches above the tallest plant** on that shelf.
- Real solution: get them outdoors. Sun is 5–10× more intense than grow lights and builds stronger stems naturally.

## Hardening-Off Checklist

Don't take seedlings from indoor nursery to direct outdoor sun in one move — they'll sunburn and shock. Instead, **harden off over 5–7 days**:

- [ ] **Day 1:** 1 hour outdoors in shade.
- [ ] **Day 2:** 2 hours outdoors, partial shade.
- [ ] **Day 3:** 3–4 hours outdoors, partial sun (morning OK).
- [ ] **Day 4:** Half day outdoors, including some direct sun.
- [ ] **Day 5:** Full day outdoors, dappled sun. Bring in for night.
- [ ] **Day 6:** Full day + leave out overnight if temps stay above 60°F.
- [ ] **Day 7:** Transplant.

> ⚠️ Skip hardening only if seedlings have already been exposed to high heat in the nursery (Houston garage heat counts) AND the transplant date has cooler weather forecast.

## Transplant-Day Protocol (per plant)

1. **Water the seedling tray** an hour before — easier extraction, less root damage.
2. **Dig the planting hole** to the depth of the cell + 1" extra. For tomatoes/peppers/jalapeños: dig deeper and bury part of the stem (they grow extra roots from buried stem = stronger plant).
3. **Drop in 1 Tbsp Trifecta Myco Supreme** at the bottom of the hole.
4. **Pop the cell out gently.** If roots are circling the bottom, loosen them with fingers or a chopstick.
5. **Set the root ball on top of the Myco** — roots in direct contact with the inoculant.
6. **Backfill with soil**, firming around the base. Don't pack too hard.
7. **Water deeply** — soak until water runs out the bottom.
8. **Mulch** with 2–3" straw, keeping a 1" gap around the stem.
9. **For tomatoes, peppers, jalapeños:** stake or cage immediately. Don't wait until they need it — driving stakes later damages roots.
10. **For corn:** plant in the cross pattern (1 center + 4 around at 8" from center) per [[Outdoor Garden Layout]]. Two containers touching each other for cross-pollination.
11. **For watermelon:** single vine in the 15–20 gal container by the trellis. Train the main vine up the trellis as it grows (loose ties).
12. **For eggshell-planted seedlings (peppers, tomatoes, jalapeños):** crack the shell more around the sides and **plant the whole thing** — eggshell decomposes into calcium, prevents blossom-end rot.

## Outdoor Direct-Sow Items

Don't try to transplant these — direct sow outdoors instead:

- **Carrots** — taproot can't tolerate disturbance. Sow into the 5-gal carrot bucket once outdoor highs settle.
- **Okra** — hates transplant shock. Soak seeds overnight, direct sow when soil hits 65°F+ consistently.
- **Cucumber** — direct sow in own pot or open spot. Trellis ready.
- **Basil** — can transplant from nursery, but direct sow also works fine and avoids fragile-stem damage.

## What Happens After Transplant

- **Day 1–3:** Seedlings often look droopy from transplant shock. Don't panic — this is normal.
- **Day 4–7:** Visibly perking up; new growth begins.
- **Week 2:** Roots establishing into surrounding soil + Myco network forming.
- **Week 3+:** Active growth phase. Start fertilizing if needed (compost tea or fish emulsion — avoid synthetic fertilizers for ~6 weeks to let mycorrhizae establish).
