> Patio garden design for [[Garden Monitor]] — raised bed, corn containers, watermelon trellis, herb stand, lime tree, and potato tower. Houston stone patio, southwest-facing house wall.

## Site

- Stone patio against the house wall. Wall faces **230° (southwest)** — see [[Patio Orientation]] for the heat-management implications.
- Hose spigot accessible at one end of the patio (future drip irrigation tap point).
- Raised bed sits on stone, not soil — drainage layer matters.

## Raised Bed (8' × 2' × 16–18" deep, galvanized steel)

Already built and placed against the house wall. Mulched with straw, ready for transplants.

**Plant placements (best 2 of each variety, transplanted at 4–6 true leaves):**

- 2 × Tomato (Beefsteak) — 24" apart, with cage or stake each. Place at the east end where they'll catch the least afternoon-wall heat.
- 2 × Bell Pepper (CA Wonder) — 18" apart, middle.
- 2 × Jalapeño (Gigante) — 18" apart, middle near peppers (same family, similar needs).
- 8 × Onion (Evergreen Bunching) — tucked between the larger plants (tiny footprint).
- Basil — between tomatoes (companion plant; supposedly improves flavor + repels some pests).
- ~~Broccoli~~ — Houston summer kills heading. Save seedlings for fall replant.

**Planting hole protocol:** 1 Tbsp Trifecta Myco Supreme into each hole. Set root ball on top, backfill. Don't combine with Miracle-Gro synthetic fertilizer (kills the fungi). Use compost tea or fish emulsion instead.

**Why this layout:**
- 2 ft wide max — reachable from the front (back is against the wall).
- 12" minimum depth required for tomato/pepper roots; 16–18" is even better in Houston summer when shallow soil cooks.
- Landscape fabric on the bottom of the bed before filling — prevents soil washing through onto the patio while still allowing drainage.

## Soil Calculation

**16 cubic feet** needed for a 2' × 8' × 12" bed. Add 15% for settling = ~18 cu ft.

| Bag size | # bags |
|---|---|
| 2 cu ft (standard) | 8–10 |
| 1.5 cu ft | 11–12 |

**Cost-saving mix recipe** (instead of all premium potting):
- 40% topsoil (cheap, ~$3/bag)
- 40% compost (~$5/bag)
- 20% perlite (~$5/bag)

→ Total ~$40–60 vs $70–130 for all-premium. Existing Miracle-Gro can fill in as part of the compost portion (just not in spots that will get Myco Supreme later).

## Corn Containers

Two large circular planters (~18–20" diameter) sitting on grass off the patio.

- **5 stalks per container in a cross pattern** — 1 in center, 4 around at ~8" from center.
- 10 plants total across both pots — tight for wind pollination but workable since the containers are touching each other.
- When tassels appear, **shake them gently each morning** to drop pollen onto silks below.
- Mulch with **2–3 inches of straw**, leaving a 1" gap around each stalk (no stem rot).

## Watermelon

**One vine only**, trained vertically up a trellis against the house wall. In its own 15–20 gallon container (or 5-gallon bucket minimum) — NOT in the raised bed.

**Why not the raised bed:** watermelon is one of the heaviest feeders + drinkers in gardening. Roots spread 2–3 ft wide; would invade tomato/pepper space within weeks and stress everything.

**Vertical training:** trellis leaning against the wall behind the pot. Vine climbs; fruit hangs in mesh slings. Yield: 2–3 melons (5–8 lbs each) — plenty for one household.

## Existing Plants (Stay in Place)

- **Herb stand** (wicker frame): lavender box, citronella pot, spearmint pot.
- **Potato tower** (clothes hamper, layered soil/hay system) — already producing top growth and side-hole sprouts. Add another soil/hay layer on top as plants grow taller (more tubers per buried stem section).
- **Lime tree** (kaffir/citrus) — healthy, may flower this season. Eventually upgrade to a 15–20 gallon pot.
- **Carrot** — 5-gallon bucket with drilled drain holes (carrots need 12+ inches of depth).
- **Oregano + Rosemary** — terracotta pots once nursery-started seedlings are ready.

## Mulching

Apply **2–3 inches of straw mulch everywhere** — including the raised bed before planting. Houston sun on bare soil hits 130°F+ at the surface and cooks shallow roots. Mulch keeps soil 15–20°F cooler and roughly halves watering frequency. Pull mulch back ~1" from each plant's stem to prevent rot.

## Companion Planting

- **Tomatoes + peppers + basil + onions** = great mix (basil deters some pests; onions repel some too).
- **Corn separated from tomatoes by 10–15 ft minimum** — they share a pest (Helicoverpa zea = corn earworm = tomato fruitworm). Adjacent planting = buffet line.
- **Watermelon kept in its own container** — too greedy to share.

## Phase 2 — Drip Irrigation

The hose spigot becomes the input for the [[Outdoor Watering System]] drip irrigation. A solenoid valve on the spigot, controlled by [[Arduino Mega]], feeds drip lines to:
- Raised bed (per-zone via subdivided manifold).
- Both corn containers.
- Watermelon container.
- Existing herb pots.

Each line gets [[Capacitive Soil Moisture Sensor|moisture sensors]] feeding [[Arduino Mega]]. Logic per zone documented in [[Outdoor Watering System]].
